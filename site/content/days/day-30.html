Today is a celebration and a next-step choice. Show your project privately to someone you trust, or keep exploring it on your own. Sharing online is optional and needs a trusted adult's agreement.

Tell someone what you made

Try saying what your project helps with, how it works, and one thing you learned while making it. Use words you would use when explaining it to a curious friend.

You do not need a resume, an interview, or a public portfolio. A private drawing, a short note, or a conversation with someone you trust is enough.

What matters is being able to explain one thing you made and one thing you discovered while making it.

Questions are better than memorized answers

You do not need to memorize answers. Choose one question about your project that you still wonder about, then think about how you might investigate it.

You do not need a perfect explanation. It is fine to say what you understand, what you are unsure about, and what you might test next.

Try explaining it out loud, or draw a picture. If you get stuck, that is a useful clue about what to explore next—not a score.

So: curiosity gives you a next step.

Choose what sounds interesting next

You might explore one idea more deeply, try a new topic, or take a break and return later. All are reasonable choices.

Write down one question you would enjoy investigating. You do not need a deadline or a finished product.

The build, step by step

  1. Draw your project or explain it in a few sentences.
  2. Write down one thing that surprised you and one thing you might change.
  3. Choose a question you want to explore next, or celebrate and stop here.
  4. Keep your project notes private, or ask a trusted adult to review them before sharing.
  5. Choose one question you want to explore next, then draw or write down how you might investigate it.
  6. Choose whether you want to explore another idea, revisit an earlier lesson, or stop here.

Where people get stuck

  • Thinking the project must be perfect. It is allowed to be small, unfinished, private, or a little silly.
  • Thinking you must keep going. You can pause, revisit an earlier idea, or finish when you are satisfied.
