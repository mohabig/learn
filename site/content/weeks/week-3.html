This week you let your project use a few small tools, such as a calculator or a search through your approved notes. A model can suggest an action, but your program decides which actions are allowed. Begin with pretend examples and tools that cannot delete, send, buy, or publish anything.

You will also meet the word agent: a program that can ask for a tool, look at its result, and choose what to do next. Put a clear limit on how many steps it may take. A helpful program should know when to stop.

Some text may contain instructions that try to trick a model. We will experiment safely with made-up examples and learn that no clever prompt or fence can guarantee perfect protection. Real security comes from limits in the surrounding program, careful testing, and adult guidance.

At the end, explain what your tool can do, what it cannot do, and how you tested it. Those are better questions than “Did I build the fanciest thing?”
